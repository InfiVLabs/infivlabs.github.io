Choosing an untangle game: what to compare before you install

Choosing an untangle game? Compare 2D and 3D rope puzzles, timers and lives, how ads are placed and offline play, with checks you can run in minutes.

Updated 26 Nov 20263 min read

The untangle games on the app stores look alike in screenshots but play very differently. Before you pick one, compare five things: whether it is a 2D crossing puzzle or a 3D stacking game, whether it uses timers or lives, where and how often ads appear, whether it works offline, and whether every level can actually be solved. Each of these can be checked in a few minutes.

Two kinds of rope game

2D crossing puzzle3D stacking game
GoalMove rope ends until no two ropes crossLift or unhook ropes in the right order until the pile is clear
What makes it hardSeeing which ropes cross and where to move themWorking out which rope is on top
ViewFlat board seen from aboveAngled camera, often rotatable
Feels likeA logic puzzleA physics toy
Common frustrationBig boards get busyRopes hidden behind each other; the camera angle hides what is on top

Neither is better. If you enjoy reasoning about a picture, the flat crossing puzzle fits. If you like rope physics and a tactile pull, the 3D games do that well. Many listings use the same words ("untangle", "untie", "knot"), so the screenshots are the quickest way to tell them apart.

Timers, lives and move limits

A calm rope puzzle and a pressured one can look identical in the first screenshot. Check the top bar of every screenshot for a clock, hearts or a moves-left counter, and search the reviews for "timer" and "lives". The full checklist is in how to find an untangle game with no timer.

How the ads are placed

Almost every free puzzle game has ads. What differs is where they land, and that matters more than whether they exist:

  • Between levels or mid-level? An ad on the result screen is a pause. An ad that interrupts a board breaks your concentration.
  • How often? Some games show one after every level from the first; others wait until you have played a while, and space them out.
  • Rewarded or forced? A rewarded ad you choose to watch for a hint is different from a full-screen ad you cannot avoid.
  • Is there a one-time purchase to remove them? Compare that price with any subscription or "VIP" offer.

On Google Play, an app with ads shows a "Contains ads" label on its listing, and an app that sells in-app purchases says so near the price or Install button. Neither label tells you how often ads appear, so the one-star reviews remain the best evidence: search them for "ads".

Does it work offline?

Relaxing offline puzzle games are popular for flights, commutes and places with weak signal. A store listing that says "offline" is a start. To be sure:

  1. Install the game and play two or three levels on a connection.
  2. Turn on airplane mode and close the game fully.
  3. Open it again and play a new level.

If the levels load and your progress saves, it works offline. Some games load fine offline but need a connection for daily challenges or events.

Can every level be solved?

Rope puzzles built by randomly scattering ropes can produce boards that have no solution, or that need far more moves than the game suggests. Games that build each level by starting from a solved board and tangling it with legal moves always have an answer. Listings rarely say which method they use, but reviews that say a level is "impossible" are a warning sign.

Comfort and controls

Small details matter over hundreds of levels:

  • Drag or tap? Being able to tap an end and then a peg helps on small phones and for anyone with shaky hands.
  • Colors. Ropes told apart by color alone are hard for color-blind players. Patterns or labels help.
  • Undo. Free undo makes experimenting painless. Some games limit it or tie it to coins.
  • Dark mode and sound settings for evening play.

Where Unskein fits

Unskein is a 2D crossing puzzle seen from above, so if you want 3D rope physics, it is not the one for you. It has no timer, no lives and no move limit, works offline, and every knot is made by tangling a solved board, so it always has an answer. It does show ads: one can appear between levels, never in the first ten and never in the middle of a knot, and a one-time purchase removes them. Controls include drag or tap, patterned ropes for color-blind players and left-handed tools. If you are new to crossing puzzles, start with how to solve rope untangle puzzles.
