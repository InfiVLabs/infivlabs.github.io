Ramadan 2027: expected start date, fasting hours and suhoor and iftar planning

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin on Monday 8 February, subject to moon sighting. Fasting hours by city, Eid al-Fitr dates and suhoor and iftar tips.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

Ramadan 2027 (1448 AH) is expected to begin on Monday 8 February 2027, with the first fast that day, if the crescent moon is sighted after sunset on Sunday 7 February. Countries that rely on a local sighting may start on Tuesday 9 February instead. Eid al-Fitr is expected on Tuesday 9 or Wednesday 10 March 2027, depending on whether the month lasts 29 or 30 days.

These dates are astronomical forecasts, subject to moon sighting. Your mosque or national authority announces the actual start, so follow your local mosque. This page is not religious or medical advice.

The 8 February forecast follows Saudi Arabia's Umm al-Qura calendar and the UAE's astronomers. Forecasts for Pakistan, India and Bangladesh point to a start on 9 February, which moves the end of the month a day later too, and India's 2027 government holiday list puts Eid al-Fitr on Wednesday 10 March.

How long are the fasts in Ramadan 2027?

A Ramadan fast runs from dawn (Fajr) to sunset (Maghrib), so its length depends on your latitude and the time of year. Ramadan 2027 falls in February and early March, close to the March equinox, so fasts are moderate almost everywhere: from about 10 hours 40 minutes to about 15 hours on the first day, closing in on 13 to 14 hours by the end of the month.

In the northern hemisphere the days are lengthening, so fasts grow by more than an hour over the month. In the southern hemisphere it is late summer, so fasts start long and get shorter.

These are our own calculations from the Sun's position, with Fajr taken when the Sun is 18 degrees below the horizon and Maghrib at sunset. Timetables that use 15 degrees for Fajr, as many in North America do, start the fast about 15 to 20 minutes later, and many mosques add a few minutes of precaution. Treat the table as a guide to how long the day will feel, and use your mosque's timetable for the actual suhoor and iftar times.

Each year Ramadan comes about 11 days earlier, so for people in Europe and North America the fasts will keep getting shorter for the next few years.

Planning suhoor

Suhoor is the meal before Fajr, and it carries you through the day.

  • Don't skip it. Health charities that write Ramadan guidance stress this, especially for anyone with diabetes.
  • Choose slow, filling starches: oats, wholegrain or high-fibre cereals, brown rice, with lentils, chickpeas or beans, yoghurt and fruit.
  • Drink well, and favour water and fluid-rich foods. Very salty food makes you thirstier during the day.
  • Set the alarm with time to spare. Eating 30 to 45 minutes before Fajr leaves time to eat, drink and pray without rushing.

Planning iftar

  • Start with water and something light with natural sugars: dates, fruit or soup.
  • Then eat a balanced meal with starchy food, vegetables, protein and some dairy. Go easy on deep-fried, creamy and very sweet foods.
  • Keep drinking between iftar and bed to make up the fluid lost in the day.
  • Expect a caffeine dip in the first days if you usually drink tea or coffee in the morning. The headaches tend to ease as your body adjusts.

Who does not have to fast

Fasting is not required of children before puberty, and people who are ill, elderly, travelling, pregnant, breastfeeding or menstruating are generally exempt or may postpone their fasts. What applies to you, and how to make up missed fasts (qada) or give fidya, is a question for your imam or a scholar you trust.

If you have diabetes or another long-term condition, talk to your doctor or diabetes team before Ramadan, ideally weeks ahead, because medicines and doses often need changing. Diabetes UK advises breaking the fast if your blood sugar drops below 4 mmol/L, and breaking it with water and getting medical advice if it climbs above the level your team gave you.

Common mistakes

  • Copying another city's timetable. Fajr and Maghrib move by minutes between nearby cities and by hours between countries.
  • Assuming the start date before the announcement. The first fast can be a day later than the forecast where the sighting is local.
  • Overeating at iftar. A large, heavy meal after a long fast often leaves people sluggish; eat in two smaller rounds instead.

Questions

When does Ramadan 2027 start?

It is expected to start on Monday 8 February 2027, if the crescent is sighted on the evening of Sunday 7 February. Some countries, including Pakistan and India, may begin on Tuesday 9 February. Follow your local mosque's announcement.

When is Eid al-Fitr 2027?

Tuesday 9 or Wednesday 10 March 2027, depending on the moon sighting at the end of Ramadan. Where Ramadan starts on 9 February, Eid can fall a day later.

How many hours is the fast in Ramadan 2027?

Between about 10 hours 40 minutes and 15 hours on the first day, depending on where you live, and about 13 to 14 hours almost everywhere by the end of the month.

What time is iftar today?

Iftar is at Maghrib, which is sunset in your city, so it changes every day and from place to place. Check your mosque's timetable or a prayer-time app set to your city.
