Intermittent fasting schedule: 16:8 and the other common plans, with examples

Intermittent fasting schedule guide: how 16:8, 14:10, 18:6 and 20:4 work, sample eating windows for real days, and how to work out your own.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

An intermittent fasting schedule is a daily split between hours of fasting and hours of eating. The most common is 16:8: you fast for 16 hours and eat within an 8-hour window, for example from 12 PM to 8 PM. Many people start gentler, with 12:12 or 14:10, and some move on to 18:6 or 20:4 later. The right schedule is the one that fits your sleep, work and family meals, and that you can keep without feeling unwell.

This page explains how fasting schedules work. It is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, under 18, have diabetes, take regular medicine or have had an eating disorder, talk to a doctor before you try fasting.

16:8 intermittent fasting: schedule examples

16:8 is popular because one skipped meal, usually breakfast, does most of the work. Here are four ways to place the same 8 hours:

Your dayEating windowFastingWhat it looks like
Late riser, family dinner12 PM to 8 PM8 PM to 12 PMLunch at noon, snack mid-afternoon, dinner by 8
Office hours10 AM to 6 PM6 PM to 10 AMLate breakfast at work, early dinner
Early bird8 AM to 4 PM4 PM to 8 AMBreakfast and lunch, light early supper
Evening workout1 PM to 9 PM9 PM to 1 PMLunch, then dinner after training

Some guidance suggests finishing your last meal about three hours before bed, which favours the earlier windows. In practice, the window you can keep five or six days a week matters more than the exact hours.

A sample 16:8 day (12 PM to 8 PM)

  1. 7:00 AM: wake up, water, black coffee or plain tea.
  2. 12:00 PM: first meal. A proper lunch with protein, vegetables and a starch.
  3. 4:00 PM: a snack if you need one: fruit, yoghurt, nuts.
  4. 7:30 PM: dinner, finished by 8.
  5. 8:00 PM to 12:00 PM next day: the fast. Water, black coffee and plain tea are fine (see what breaks a fast).

How to work out your eating window

Your fast starts at your last bite, not when you go to bed. Add the fasting hours to that time and you have the moment your next window opens:

Then count the eating hours forward from your first meal to see when the window closes. On a 16:8 plan with a first meal at 12 PM, the window closes at 8 PM.

Choosing a schedule

  • New to fasting? Many people start with 12:12, which mostly means not snacking after dinner, then try 14:10 for a week or two before 16:8.
  • Shift work. Anchor the window to your waking hours, not the clock. A night-shift worker might eat from 6 PM to 2 AM.
  • Family meals. If dinner with others matters, keep it inside the window and skip breakfast instead.
  • Training. Many people prefer a window that includes a meal after exercise.
  • 18:6 and 20:4 leave less room to eat enough protein, fibre and vitamins. Move to them only once a shorter plan feels easy, if at all.
  • Women in perimenopause may notice changes in sleep or cycles; medical guidance suggests extra caution. If anything feels off, shorten the fast.

Common mistakes

  • Counting from bedtime. The fast starts at your last bite.
  • Drinking calories in the fast: milky coffee, juice, smoothies.
  • Making up for it in the window. A huge meal at the end of a fast often leaves people sluggish and hungry again soon.
  • Jumping straight to 20:4. Hunger, headaches and irritability are common side effects of going too far too fast.
  • Ignoring how you feel. Dizziness, fainting or feeling unwell are signs to eat and stop, not to push on.

Questions

What is the 16:8 intermittent fasting schedule?

You fast for 16 hours and eat within the remaining 8, every day or most days. A common version is eating from 12 PM to 8 PM and fasting from 8 PM to noon the next day.

Which intermittent fasting schedule is good for beginners?

Many people start with 12:12 or 14:10 and move to 16:8 after a week or two once the shorter fast feels easy. There is no need to go further than a schedule you can keep comfortably.

Does sleep count toward the fasting hours?

Yes. The fast runs from your last bite, so the hours you sleep are part of it. That is why most people put the fasting part across the night.

Can I drink water during intermittent fasting?

Yes. Water, sparkling water, black coffee and unsweetened tea are generally fine during the fasting hours. Drinks with calories end the fast.
