Number games for seniors: large print puzzles to enjoy

Number games for seniors that are easy to read and quick to learn: which puzzles suit older adults, how to play in large print, and tablet setup tips.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

Good number games for seniors are easy to read, quick to learn and played at your own pace. Pencil classics like Take Ten and sudoku fit that well, as do kakuro, number search and card or tile games that count to a target. What makes any of them comfortable is large, high-contrast digits, no clock ticking, and a way to undo a slip. You can play all of them on paper for nothing, or on a tablet where you control the size.

This page is about choosing and setting up games you enjoy. It is not health or eye care advice. If reading has become harder recently, an eye exam is the right first step.

What makes a number game easy to enjoy

  • Readable digits. Large type means 18 point or more on paper, by the definition used by the American Printing House for the Blind. On a screen, look for a setting that enlarges the digits themselves, not just the page.
  • Rules that fit in a sentence. The best games are learned in a minute and stay interesting for years.
  • No time pressure. A timer you can hide, or none at all.
  • Forgiving controls. Undo, and buttons big enough that a near miss does not count.
  • Short sessions that keep their place. A game you can put down for the kettle and pick up again.
  • No account. Nothing to sign up for, no password to remember.

Most of us find close print harder over time: the US National Eye Institute notes that nearly everyone develops presbyopia, trouble focusing up close, usually after age 45. Choosing a large print game is ordinary good sense, not a special need.

If you enjoy arithmetic, kakuro and cribbage keep you counting. If you would rather not do sums at all, sudoku uses digits only as symbols. Take Ten sits in between: the only fact you need is which pairs make ten.

Playing number games on a tablet

A tablet is often the most comfortable way to play. The screen is larger than a phone's, you can raise the brightness, and you never run out of puzzles or erasers.

  1. Raise the text size of the whole device. On an iPad or iPhone, go to Settings, then Display & Brightness, then Text Size. On most Android tablets and phones, go to Settings, then Accessibility, then Display size and text.
  2. Look for a size setting inside the game. Not every app follows the system text size, so a game's own large digit mode matters more.
  3. Try landscape. Many games give the board more room when the tablet lies sideways.
  4. Use dark mode in the evening. Light digits on a dark background cut glare in a dim room.
  5. Rest the tablet on a stand or cushion, so you can sit back and tap rather than hold it up.

Large print number games on paper

Paper still has a lot going for it: no screen, no battery, and the satisfaction of a pencil stroke. To make it easier on the eyes:

  • For Take Ten, use squared paper with 1 cm squares and a dark felt-tip pen, one digit per square. The Take Ten pencil game rules show how to set up the starting grid.
  • When you print puzzles, choose "Actual size" in the print dialog, one puzzle per page, and the darkest print setting.
  • Work under a lamp aimed at the page rather than overhead light.

Playing together

Number games travel well between generations. Take Ten is quick to teach a grandchild who knows that 3 and 7 make ten, and a pair of players can share one page, taking turns. On a phone or tablet, many games let you send the same puzzle to someone else and compare results.

Questions

What are good number games for seniors?

Take Ten, sudoku, kakuro, number search, dominoes and cribbage are all popular. Pick by how much arithmetic you enjoy: sudoku needs none, Take Ten needs only pairs that make ten, and kakuro and cribbage involve adding up.

Are there free number games for seniors?

Yes. All of the pencil games above cost nothing but paper. Many number puzzle apps are free to play and pay for themselves with ads, usually with a purchase to remove them.

What games are good for seniors on a tablet?

Puzzle games with a large digit setting, no timer pressure and undo work best. Number pair games, sudoku and word games are good fits because they can be played in short sessions.

Do you need to be good at math for number games?

No. Many number games use digits only as symbols. Take Ten asks you to know which pairs make ten, and that is all the math it needs.

How Tally Ten fits

Tally Ten is a phone and tablet version of Take Ten. Its Big Numbers mode lays the board out in six columns with digits twice the size, and you can choose it on the first screen. The page is a squared exercise book by day and a chalkboard in dark mode, and the timer, pairs-left count and vibration are all optional. It plays offline with no account, every mode is free with ads, and you can send a friend a Journey level to compare ticks. For tips on planning moves, see the number matching strategy guide.
