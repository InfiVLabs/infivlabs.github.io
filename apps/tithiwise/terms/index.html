What the app calculates, and what it does not

Tithiwise computes the panchang from astronomical positions of the sun and moon for the city you choose, using that city's own sunrise. Because a lunar day begins at the same instant everywhere but sunrise does not, a festival can fall on a different calendar date in different cities, and the app shows the reference date when it differs.

Hindu traditions differ, legitimately, on how some dates are fixed — amanta against purnimanta reckoning, Smarta against Vaishnava Ekadashi, regional and sampradaya customs, and local temple practice. Tithiwise follows the conventions described in the app and cannot represent every tradition at once. It is a calendar and a timings reference. It does not give horoscopes, predictions, remedies or any form of fortune telling, and it is not religious authority. For an observance that matters to you, confirm the date with your family practice, your temple or your priest.

Times are computed to the minute but depend on the coordinates and time zone bundled for your city, and on your phone's clock. Do not rely on the app alone where an exact moment matters.
