Choghadiya today: how to work it out for your city

Choghadiya today, worked out step by step: split daylight and night into eight, start from the weekday's period, and read which ones are good.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

Today's choghadiya comes from two numbers: your city's sunrise and sunset. Daylight is split into eight equal periods and the night into eight more; the weekday decides which of the seven choghadiyas comes first, and the rest follow in a fixed order. Amrit, Shubh and Labh are counted as good, Char as fine, and Udveg, Kaal and Rog are the ones families avoid for starting something new.

A page like this one can't show the live choghadiya for your city, because the times move every day with sunrise and sunset. What it can do is show you exactly how the table is built, so you can check any list you are given.

Choghadiya is a traditional way of choosing a time of day. This page explains the arithmetic; for a wedding, griha pravesh or other important muhurat, ask your family priest.

The seven choghadiyas

The seven always follow each other in the same order through the day: Udveg, Char, Labh, Amrit, Kaal, Shubh, Rog, and back to Udveg. Each is linked in tradition to a planet (Udveg to the Sun, Amrit to the Moon, Rog to Mars, Labh to Mercury, Shubh to Jupiter, Char to Venus, Kaal to Saturn), and the weekday's own planet opens the day. In Gujarati the word is ચોઘડિયા, in Hindi चौघड़िया.

How choghadiya is worked out, step by step

  1. Find sunrise, sunset and the next sunrise for your city. Use the actual times for the date, not 6 AM and 6 PM.
  2. Divide daylight by eight. Sunset minus sunrise, divided by 8, is the length of each day choghadiya. In winter they are shorter than 90 minutes; in summer, longer.
  3. Divide the night by eight. Next sunrise minus sunset, divided by 8, gives the night choghadiyas.
  4. Start the day with the weekday's choghadiya from the table below, then step through the order above.
  5. Start the night with the weekday's night choghadiya. At night each period jumps five places along the same order instead of one: after Shubh comes Amrit, then Char, then Rog.
WeekdayFirst day choghadiyaFirst night choghadiya
SundayUdvegShubh
MondayAmritChar
TuesdayRogKaal
WednesdayLabhUdveg
ThursdayShubhAmrit
FridayCharRog
SaturdayKaalLabh

The last day choghadiya is always the same as the first, because eight periods go round a cycle of seven.

A worked example

Say it is a Thursday, sunrise is 6:40 AM, sunset is 6:00 PM, and the next sunrise is 6:40 AM. These are round numbers for the example, not a real city's times.

Daylight is 11 hours 20 minutes, or 680 minutes, so each day choghadiya lasts 85 minutes. Thursday starts with Shubh:

Day choghadiyaTimeQuality
Shubh6:40 to 8:05 AMGood
Rog8:05 to 9:30 AMAvoid
Udveg9:30 to 10:55 AMAvoid
Char10:55 AM to 12:20 PMFine
Labh12:20 to 1:45 PMGood
Amrit1:45 to 3:10 PMGood
Kaal3:10 to 4:35 PMAvoid
Shubh4:35 to 6:00 PMGood

The night is 12 hours 40 minutes, or 760 minutes, so each night choghadiya lasts 95 minutes. Thursday night starts with Amrit and steps five places each time: Amrit (6:00 to 7:35 PM), Char, Rog, Kaal, Labh, Udveg, Shubh, and Amrit again (5:05 to 6:40 AM).

So if someone asks for a good time to leave on a trip that Thursday morning, Char at 10:55 AM or Labh from 12:20 PM would be the usual answer, and 8:05 to 10:55 AM would be avoided.

Rahu kaal is a separate check

Rahu kaal also comes from splitting daylight into eight, but it is one fixed eighth per weekday: the 2nd on Monday, 3rd on Saturday, 4th on Friday, 5th on Wednesday, 6th on Thursday, 7th on Tuesday and 8th on Sunday. On the Thursday above it is the 6th eighth, 1:45 to 3:10 PM, which overlaps the Amrit choghadiya. Many families skip a good choghadiya when Rahu kaal falls inside it.

Common mistakes

  • Using fixed 90-minute blocks. That only works on a day with exactly 12 hours of daylight. In Delhi in December, a day choghadiya lasts about 78 minutes.
  • Using India's times abroad. A choghadiya list printed for Ahmedabad is wrong in London or Houston. The order is the same, but every time shifts with your sunrise and sunset.
  • Switching weekday at midnight. The Hindu day runs from sunrise to sunrise, so at 2 AM on a Friday you are still in Thursday's night choghadiyas.
  • Forgetting the night list. After sunset the day table no longer applies.

Questions

What is choghadiya?

A traditional division of the day into eight periods from sunrise to sunset and eight from sunset to the next sunrise, each named after one of seven choghadiyas (Amrit, Shubh, Labh, Char, Udveg, Kaal, Rog). It is used especially in Gujarat and western India to pick a time to start work, travel or a purchase.

How long is one choghadiya?

About 90 minutes on average, but never exactly fixed: it is one eighth of the day's daylight, or one eighth of the night. In winter day choghadiyas are shorter and night ones longer.

Which choghadiya is good today?

Amrit, Shubh and Labh are the good ones, and Char is fine, especially for travel. Which of them falls when depends on the weekday and your city's sunrise, so work it out with the steps above or check a panchang set to your city.

Is aaj ka choghadiya the same in every city?

The order of choghadiyas is the same everywhere on the same weekday, but the times are not. They follow local sunrise and sunset, so Mumbai and Delhi differ by several minutes, and cities abroad differ completely.
