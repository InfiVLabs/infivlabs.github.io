Tithiwise: a Hindu calendar and panchang for the city you live in

Tithi, choghadiya and festival dates for your own city and sunrise, offline

In closed testing

Tithiwise is a Hindu calendar and panchang app for Android. Open it and the first screen answers the questions most people check every morning: which tithi it is today and the minute it ends, which choghadiya is running now and whether it is a good one, and when Rahu kaal falls. Everything is worked out on the phone for the city you pick, so it works offline.

What you can do with it

  • Read today's panchang. Tithi, nakshatra, yoga and karana with their end times, paksha, the month in both amanta and purnimanta reckoning, sunrise, sunset, moonrise and moonset, Brahma muhurat, Abhijit, Rahu kaal, Yamaganda and Gulika. Tap any term to see what it means.
  • See the whole day on one line. A ruler runs from sunrise to the next sunrise with every choghadiya at its real length. Each period says "Good", "Fine" or "Avoid" in words, and a needle marks where you are now.
  • Keep track of vrats and festivals. About 50 yearly festivals, every Ekadashi under its own name, Purnima, Amavasya, Sankashti, Pradosh, Masik Shivratri and Vinayaka Chaturthi, for this year and next. Festival pages give the puja time, the time to break an Ekadashi fast, and the Vaishnava date when it differs.
  • Get a reminder the evening before. Pick the observances you keep. Nothing arrives between 10 PM and 6 AM.
  • Browse the month. A ruled grid shows each date's tithi, festivals in red and the moon on Purnima and Amavasya.
  • Share and glance. A home-screen widget shows today's tithi and the next festival, and a "Today's panchang" card can be sent to the family group as an image.

The app is in English, Hindi, Marathi and Gujarati.

Who it is for

Families who keep vrats and want the date right without reading a dense table, and Indian families abroad. A tithi starts and ends at the same moment everywhere, but sunrise does not, so a festival or an Ekadashi can land on a different day in New Jersey or Toronto than in India. Tithiwise computes each date for your own sunrise and says so when it differs from India ("In New Delhi it falls on..."). It bundles 326 cities across India, the US, the UK, Canada, the Gulf and other places where Indian families live.

How it differs

It is a calendar and timings only: no horoscopes, predictions or remedies. There is no account and no location permission; you choose a place from the list and your data stays on your phone. The panchang, festivals and reminders are open to everyone. Banner ads appear on the Month and Festivals tabs; Tithiwise Plus, yearly or lifetime, removes them and lets you save as many cities as you like.

Dates can differ between traditions and regions. For a vrat or a puja, follow your temple or family priest.

Questions

Is Tithiwise a panchang or a calendar?

Both. The Today screen is a daily panchang (tithi, nakshatra, yoga, karana and the day's timings), and the Month and Festivals tabs work as a Hindu calendar for the year.

Does it work outside India?

Yes. Pick your city and every date and time is calculated for its sunrise. See why Ekadashi falls on a different day in the USA.

Does it show Smarta and Vaishnava Ekadashi?

It gives the Ekadashi date for your city and notes the Vaishnava (ISKCON) date on the days it differs, along with the time to break the fast.

Does it work without internet?

Yes. The calculations run on the phone, so the panchang, festivals and reminders all work offline.
