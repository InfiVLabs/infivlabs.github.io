Gratitude journal prompts: 60 questions for adults

60 gratitude journal prompts for adults, for mornings, evenings and hard days, plus how to keep a gratitude journal so it doesn't turn into a chore.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

A gratitude journal prompt is a short, specific question that makes it easy to write one thing you're thankful for, such as "Who made today a little easier?" Specific prompts work better than "What am I grateful for?", which tends to produce the same three answers every day. Below are 60 prompts in six groups, then a few notes on making the habit stick.

Morning gratitude prompts

  1. What are you looking forward to today, however small?
  2. What is one comfort you woke up with this morning?
  3. Who will you see today that you're glad to have in your life?
  4. What did your sleep give you, even if it wasn't perfect?
  5. What is something in your home that makes mornings easier?
  6. What is one thing your body can do today that you're thankful for?
  7. What went right yesterday that you didn't notice at the time?
  8. What is a skill you'll use today that you're glad you have?
  9. What part of your routine do you actually enjoy?
  10. What would make today good enough?

Evening gratitude prompts

  1. Who made today a little easier?
  2. Which small thing went right today?
  3. Which moment today felt calm?
  4. What made you smile, even for a second?
  5. What did you eat today that you enjoyed?
  6. What did you finish today?
  7. What did you learn today, however small?
  8. What is something kind you did today?
  9. What kindness did you notice today?
  10. What problem turned out smaller than you thought?

Prompts about people

  1. Who would you thank if you could, right now?
  2. Which friend do you appreciate this week, and why?
  3. Who taught you something useful once?
  4. Who texted or called that you were glad to hear from?
  5. Who do you trust with bad news?
  6. Who makes you laugh without trying?
  7. What did someone say that stayed with you?
  8. Who helped you in a way they probably don't remember?
  9. Which teacher, coach or boss shaped how you work?
  10. Who would you like to spend more time with, and what do you like about them?

Prompts about small, everyday things

  1. What sound did you like hearing today?
  2. What smell reminded you of something good?
  3. Which song fitted your day?
  4. What did you see outside today that was nice?
  5. What is something you own that makes life easier?
  6. Which meal this week would you happily have again?
  7. What is a place you're glad exists?
  8. What made your commute or walk better?
  9. What is a comfort you often forget about?
  10. What did the weather give you today?

Prompts about yourself

  1. What is something you're proud of this week?
  2. What did you do today just for yourself?
  3. What are you glad you said no to?
  4. What are you glad you said yes to?
  5. What is a habit that's quietly helping you?
  6. What is something your body did well today?
  7. What is a mistake that taught you something useful?
  8. What is one thing about you that a friend would thank you for?
  9. What did you handle better than you would have a year ago?
  10. What part of your life would your younger self be pleased about?

Prompts for hard days

On a bad day, "be grateful" can feel like being told to cheer up. These prompts are smaller on purpose.

  1. What helped you get through a hard moment today?
  2. What is one thing that didn't go wrong?
  3. Who could you call if you needed to?
  4. What did you get to rest from today?
  5. What is one thing you can look forward to tomorrow?
  6. What is a basic need that was met today: food, warmth, a bed?
  7. What is a hard thing you've gotten through before?
  8. What would you say to a friend who had your day?
  9. What is one tiny thing you did to look after yourself?
  10. What made today a little less hard than it could have been?

How to keep a gratitude journal

  • Write one line, not a page. One specific sentence takes 30 seconds, which makes it easy to keep going.
  • Be specific. "My sister sent a voice note while I was cooking" beats "my family". Details make you relive the moment.
  • Pick a fixed time. Tie it to something you already do: coffee, brushing your teeth, plugging in your phone.
  • Rotate prompts. Use a different prompt each day, so the answers don't become automatic.
  • Don't force it on hard days. Use the hard-day prompts, or skip. A gap is fine.
  • Reread now and then. A month of lines is a good thing to read on a bad evening.

Does a gratitude journal help?

Research suggests it can help some people feel better. In a well-known set of studies (Emmons and McCullough, 2003), people randomly assigned to list things they were grateful for reported higher well-being on several, though not all, measures than comparison groups; the effect on positive mood was the most consistent. It is a small, low-cost practice, not a treatment, and it won't fix every bad week.

Questions

What should I write in a gratitude journal?

Write one to three specific things from your day that you're thankful for, and why. A person, a moment, a meal or a small win all count. A prompt helps you find something new.

How often should you write in a gratitude journal?

Whatever you can keep up. Some people write one line every evening; others write a few lines once or twice a week. Consistency matters more than length.

What are good morning gratitude prompts?

Questions that look ahead or back to yesterday, such as "What are you looking forward to today?" or "What went right yesterday that you didn't notice at the time?" The ten morning prompts above are a good start.

What if I can't think of anything to be grateful for?

Go smaller: a warm drink, a bed, a text from someone. On very hard days, it's fine to skip. If most days feel like that, talk to someone you trust or a doctor.
