Potty training a puppy

Potty training a puppy step by step: when to take them out, a simple schedule, night trips, handling accidents without punishment, and when to ask a vet.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

Potty training a puppy comes down to timing: take your puppy outside before they need to go, wait at the same spot, and reward the moment they finish, right there outside. Go out first thing after waking, after every meal, drink and nap, during and after play, and before bed. Clean accidents without a word. Scolding doesn't teach a puppy where to go; it teaches them to hide when they need to.

When to take your puppy out

Young puppies can't hold it for long, so the trick is to be outside at the moments they are most likely to go:

  • The moment they wake up, from the night or from a nap. Carry a young puppy if the walk to the door is long.
  • After eating. The American Kennel Club suggests going out between 5 and 30 minutes after a meal, sooner for younger puppies.
  • After drinking.
  • During and after play, especially when play stops suddenly.
  • When you see the signs: sniffing the floor, circling, whining, or heading for a corner or the door.
  • Last thing before bed.

Between those moments, go out at least every two hours while your puppy is awake. A common rule of thumb is that a puppy can hold it for at most their age in months plus one hour: about three hours for a two-month-old. Treat that as the upper limit, not the plan.

Step by step: one potty trip

  1. Go out with your puppy, on a leash if your yard isn't fenced. You need to be there to reward at the right moment.
  2. Walk to the same spot every time. The smell helps your puppy remember what this place is for.
  3. Stand still and be boring. No play, no chatting. Give it three to five minutes.
  4. Say your cue quietly as they start, such as "go potty". Over time the word helps them go on request.
  5. Mark and reward right there. Click or say "yes" as they finish, then treat immediately, outside. A treat back indoors rewards coming inside, not peeing.
  6. Play a little afterward. If going out always ends the fun, some puppies learn to hold it to stay outside longer.
  7. Nothing happened? Go back in, keep your puppy close or in their crate or pen for 10 to 15 minutes, and try again.

Potty training at night

  • Crate or bed close to you. If you can hear your puppy stir, you can get them out before an accident.
  • Make the last trip as late as practical, and keep the evening calm so your puppy settles quickly.
  • Night trips are business only. Lights low, no play, no chat, straight back to bed. A fun 3 a.m. trip teaches your puppy to wake you for fun.
  • Set an alarm at first if your puppy can't yet last the night, rather than waiting for crying. As they grow, push the alarm later and then drop it.

How to handle accidents

Accidents will happen. How you react decides whether they slow you down.

  • Caught in the act? Calmly interrupt (a quiet "oops"), scoop your puppy up and take them to their spot. If they finish outside, reward.
  • Found it afterward? Just clean it. Your puppy can't link a scolding to something they did minutes ago, and punishment makes many puppies afraid to go in front of you, even outside.
  • Clean with an enzyme cleaner made for pet urine. Ordinary cleaners can leave a smell your puppy can still find, and that draws them back to the same spot.
  • Watch the pattern. Accidents at the same time of day usually mean a trip is missing from the schedule there.

Common potty training mistakes

  • Too much freedom too soon. A young puppy loose in the whole house will find a corner. Expand their space room by room as they stay clean.
  • Rewarding late. The reward has to follow the pee within a second or two, outside.
  • Waiting for signs only. Young puppies give very little warning. Go out on the schedule, not only when asked.
  • Calling it done too early. A week without accidents is progress, not the finish line. Keep rewarding outside trips for a good while after.

When to talk to your vet

Every puppy takes their own time; some are reliable in a few weeks and many take several months. Check with your vet if your puppy pees very often or in small amounts, strains, has blood in their urine, drinks much more than usual, has diarrhea, or suddenly starts having accidents after being clean. These can have medical causes, such as a urinary tract infection, that no amount of training will fix.

This page is general guidance for healthy puppies, not veterinary advice. If anything about your puppy's toileting worries you, ask your vet.

Questions

How long does it take to potty train a puppy?

It varies a lot. Some puppies are mostly reliable within a few weeks, while many take several months, and toy breeds often need more frequent trips for longer. Consistent timing matters more than any method.

How often should I take my puppy out?

After waking, eating, drinking and playing, before bed, and at least every two hours while awake for a young puppy. As your puppy grows, the gaps can stretch.

How do I potty train a puppy at night?

Keep the crate near you, take your puppy out last thing before bed, and make any night trip quick and boring. Set an alarm at first instead of waiting for crying, and push it later as your puppy grows.

Should I punish my puppy for accidents?

No. Punishment teaches a puppy to hide when they need to go, which makes training harder. Interrupt calmly, take them outside, reward if they finish there, and clean the spot with an enzyme cleaner.
