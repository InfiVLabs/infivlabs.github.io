Puppy schedule by age: 8, 10 and 12 weeks

A puppy schedule by age with a sample daily routine for an 8 week old puppy, what changes at 10 and 12 weeks, and what to teach at each age.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

A good puppy schedule is mostly sleep, potty trips and short bursts of play, with training squeezed into the gaps. At 8 weeks, plan on a trip outside right after every nap, meal and play session and at least every two hours while awake, three or four small meals, and a lot of sleep: many young puppies sleep 16 to 20 hours a day. Training sessions last two to five minutes, several times a day. As your puppy grows toward 12 weeks, the gaps between potty trips stretch and the awake stretches get a little longer.

Sample daily schedule for an 8 week old puppy

Shift the times to fit your day. What matters is the order: wake, outside, eat, outside, play and train a little, then sleep.

TimeWhat happens
6:30 a.m.Wake up. Carry or walk your puppy straight outside, before you do anything else.
6:45 a.m.Breakfast, then back outside within a few minutes to half an hour.
7:15 a.m.Play and one short training game (2 to 3 minutes), such as the name game.
7:45 a.m.Nap in the crate or pen, about 1.5 to 2 hours.
9:30 a.m.Outside the moment they wake. Then quiet exploring and chewing.
10:30 a.m.Outside again, then nap.
NoonOutside, lunch, outside. Short play and a training game.
12:45 p.m.Long nap.
3:00 p.m.Outside, then a new-thing session: a new surface, a gentle sound, a calm visitor.
4:00 p.m.Outside, then nap.
5:30 p.m.Outside, dinner, outside. Play and a short training game.
6:30 p.m.Evening nap (this keeps the bitey "overtired" hour shorter).
8:00 p.m.Outside, calm play, handling practice (paws, ears, a brush).
10:00 p.m.Last trip outside, then bed in a crate near you.
NightExpect one or two quiet trips outside. Keep them dull: no play, no lights, straight back to bed.

The sample shows three meals. If your vet or the food's guide suggests four at this age, add a small one mid-afternoon.

Puppies also need to go out after drinking, after any excitement, and whenever you see sniffing, circling or a sudden walk away from play. Those signs override the clock.

Treat these as starting points, not targets. Toy breeds often need more frequent potty trips, and meal count and amount should follow your vet and the food's feeding guide. A common rule of thumb is that a puppy can hold their bladder for at most their age in months plus one hour; that is the maximum, not how often to go out.

What to teach a puppy at 8, 10 and 12 weeks

You can start training the day your puppy comes home. Early lessons are about routines and good associations more than commands.

AgeFocusTry
8 weeksSettling inName game, a potty routine, the crate as a nice place, gentle handling, charging the clicker
10 weeksFirst skillsSit, hand target, redirecting mouthing onto toys, a few minutes alone in a pen, new sounds at low volume
12 weeksBuilding on itDown, coming when called indoors, an introduction to "leave it", wearing a collar or harness and leash indoors, puppy class

If your puppy is struggling with something, go back an age row. A 12-week-old who just arrived needs the 8-week basics first.

Socialization: the part with a deadline

Puppies learn what is normal and safe fastest in their first three months or so, which overlaps almost entirely with these weeks. The American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior says puppies can start socialization classes as early as 7 to 8 weeks, once they have had their first vaccinations and a deworming, if they are kept up to date after that. Ask your vet what fits your puppy and your area.

Good socialization means calm, pleasant experiences, not lots of them. Let your puppy watch from a distance, pay with treats, and leave before they get worried. Busy places where unknown dogs gather, such as dog parks, are best avoided until your vet says the vaccine course is done.

Common schedule mistakes

  • Too much awake time. An overtired puppy bites more and listens less. If the evening turns wild, the fix is usually a nap.
  • Long training sessions. Two minutes that end on a success beat fifteen minutes that end in frustration.
  • Playing at night trips. Night trips should be boring, or your puppy learns that 3 a.m. is party time.
  • Leaving water and food to chance. Regular meal times make potty times predictable.
  • Copying another puppy's schedule exactly. Your puppy's own rhythm, which you will see within a few days, beats any table.

This page is general guidance for healthy puppies, not veterinary advice. Ask your vet about feeding amounts, vaccinations and when to go out in public. If your puppy has diarrhea, isn't eating, seems unusually sleepy, or suddenly starts having accidents after being reliable, call your vet.

Questions

What should an 8 week old puppy's schedule look like?

Mostly sleep, with short awake windows of under an hour. Take your puppy out on waking, after meals and after play, feed three or four small meals, and fit in a few training games of two or three minutes each.

How long can a 10 week old puppy go between potty breaks?

During the day, plan on about every two hours while awake, plus after every nap, meal and play session. The "months plus one" rule is a maximum, and many puppies need to go sooner.

What should I teach my puppy first?

Their name, a potty routine and that the crate is a good place. Sit and a hand target come next. These make every later lesson easier.

How long should puppy training sessions be?

Two to five minutes, several times a day. Stop while your puppy still wants more.
