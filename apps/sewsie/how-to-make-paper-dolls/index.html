How to make paper dolls and clothes with kids

How to make paper dolls and clothes at home: what paper to use, how to draw the doll, fold-over tabs that hold, a stand, and wardrobe ideas by season.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

To make a paper doll, draw a simple figure on thin card, standing straight with arms held a little away from the body, and cut it out. To make clothes, lay plain paper over the doll, trace the outline of the garment, then add small rectangles at the shoulders and sides before cutting. Those rectangles are tabs: fold them behind the doll and the clothes stay on. A whole doll with three outfits takes about an hour, and children can design every piece themselves.

What you need

  • Thin card for the doll: a cereal box, an old greeting card or cardstock. It must be stiff enough to stand and survive many changes.
  • Plain printer paper for the clothes: thin paper folds cleanly at the tabs.
  • Pencil, eraser, crayons or colored pencils, felt-tip pens.
  • Child-safe scissors. For children under about five, a grown-up does the cutting while the child draws and colors.
  • Optional: a clothespin or a strip of card for a stand, glue stick, scraps of wrapping paper or fabric.

How to draw the paper doll

  1. Draw a stick figure first. Head, a line for the body, two lines for arms angled down and out, two lines for legs slightly apart. This is the pose; it decides what clothes will fit.
  2. Keep arms away from the body. Arms that touch the sides make sleeves and coats almost impossible to cut.
  3. Build shapes around the lines. An oval head, a neck, a rectangle-ish body slightly narrower at the waist, tube arms and legs, simple feet pointing outward.
  4. Make it big. A doll about 20 cm (8 inches) tall is easy for small hands to dress. Tiny dolls mean tiny tabs that tear.
  5. Add a face and hair, then go over the outline with a dark pen so it is easy to cut.
  6. Cut it out with a small margin around fiddly parts like fingers. A mitten-shaped hand is fine.

Children can make the doll look like themselves: skin tone, hair color, glasses, freckles. That is often the part they care about most.

How to make paper doll clothes with tabs

  1. Lay paper over the doll and lightly trace the area the garment covers: for a dress, from the shoulders to the knees.
  2. Draw the garment a few millimeters outside the traced line so it covers the doll fully.
  3. Add tabs before cutting: small rectangles, about the width of a fingertip, sticking out at both shoulders and at each side of the waist or hem.
  4. Color and decorate while the piece is still flat.
  5. Cut around the garment and the tabs together. Do not cut the tabs off by mistake; color them a pale shade so they stand out.
  6. Fold the tabs back around the doll's edges.

Tabs go only where the doll has an edge to fold around: shoulders, sides of the waist, hips. A tab on a sleeve with no arm behind it will not hold.

GarmentWhere to put the tabs
Dress or coatBoth shoulders, both sides at the waist
Top or sweaterBoth shoulders, both sides at the bottom
Skirt or trousersBoth sides of the waistband
HatOne at each side of the head
Shoes or bootsOne at the outer side of each foot
Bag or scarfOne where it meets the shoulder or neck

How to make the paper doll stand

  • Clothespin stand. Clip a wooden clothespin to the bottom of the feet. It works at once and moves between dolls.
  • Slotted base. Cut a rectangle of card about 5 by 8 cm (2 by 3 inches), cut a slit halfway into it, and slide the doll's feet into the slit.
  • Fold-back stand. Glue a long, thin triangle of card to the doll's back, folded at the top, so it leans like a picture frame.

Wardrobe ideas by season

  • Winter: a party dress, a long coat, a scarf, a woolly hat and snow boots. Pair it with the palettes in Christmas dress coloring ideas.
  • Spring: a raincoat, rain boots and an umbrella with a tab at the handle.
  • Summer: a sundress, sandals and a wide sun hat.
  • Dress-up day: a ballet tutu, a crown, a superhero cape that the child invents.

Store each doll and her wardrobe in a labeled envelope so nothing gets lost under the sofa.

Make it more fun

  • Design brief cards. Write prompts on slips of paper, such as "a coat for the rain" or "a dress for a party at night", and draw one each time.
  • Fabric and paper scraps. Glue scraps of wrapping paper, felt or tissue onto plain paper clothes for texture.
  • Matching family. Make a doll for each family member and dress them for the same outing.
  • Fashion show. Line the dolls up on a shelf and let the child present each outfit.

A little history

Paper dolls with changeable clothes have been sold for more than two hundred years. The History of Little Fanny, published in London in 1810 by S. and J. Fuller, came with one movable head, seven costumes and four hats, and is widely regarded as the first published paper doll. Fanny's clothes held the head in a small pocket instead of using fold-over tabs.

Questions

What paper is best for paper dolls?

Use thin card, such as cardstock or the inside of a cereal box, for the doll, and ordinary printer paper for the clothes. Card keeps the doll standing; thin paper lets the tabs fold without cracking.

How do you make paper doll clothes stay on?

Add small rectangular tabs at the shoulders and sides when you draw the garment, cut them out with it, and fold them behind the doll. Tabs only hold where the doll has an edge to fold around.

How do you make a paper doll stand up?

Clip a clothespin to its feet, slide the feet into a slit cut in a small card base, or glue a folded card strip to its back like a picture frame.
