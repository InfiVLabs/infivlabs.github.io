How to draw a dress for kids: easy steps with simple shapes

How to draw a dress for kids in six easy steps, using simple shapes, plus five dress styles to try, coloring tips and fixes for common mistakes.

Updated 26 Nov 20264 min read

To draw a dress, start with two shapes: a small rectangle for the top (the bodice) and a big triangle or bell shape for the skirt, joined at a narrow waist. Add sleeves, a neckline and a wavy hem, then decorate. Every dress style below starts from those same two shapes, so once a child can draw them, they can design a gown, a frock or a party dress.

Draw a dress in six steps

  1. Draw the top. A small rectangle, a little wider at the shoulders than at the bottom. This is the bodice.
  2. Draw the waist. A short horizontal line under the bodice, a bit narrower than the shoulders. A ribbon or belt can sit here later.
  3. Draw the skirt. From each end of the waist, draw a line slanting outward and down. Join the bottoms with a curved or wavy line. This bell or triangle is the skirt.
  4. Add the neckline. A curve (round neck), a V shape, or a straight line across (square neck) at the top of the bodice.
  5. Add sleeves. Small half-circles at the shoulders for puff sleeves, short rectangles for cap sleeves, or long tubes for a winter dress. Leave them off for a sleeveless dress.
  6. Decorate. Draw a bow at the waist, frills at the hem, buttons down the front, or a pattern on the skirt. Then color.

A tip for small hands: draw lightly in pencil first, then go over the lines you like with a dark crayon or pen. Erase the rest.

Coloring the dress

  • Pick a main color and an accent. The skirt in the main color, the sash and trims in the accent. Two or three colors look neater than ten.
  • Color in one direction. Strokes that all go the same way, top to bottom, give a smoother fill than scribbles.
  • Add a pattern on top. After the base color, add dots, stripes, small hearts or stars with a darker pen. A plain skirt becomes fabric.
  • Shade the folds. Press a little harder near the waist and in long lines down the skirt to suggest folds.
  • Use white for shine. On dark paper, a white crayon line along a fold looks like satin.

Common mistakes and quick fixes

ProblemFix
The dress looks like a triangle with no person in itDraw the waist narrower than the shoulders and the hem
Sleeves look stuck onStart them at the very corner of the shoulder, not halfway down
The skirt is lopsidedDraw a faint line down the middle first, then make both sides match
No room for the skirtStart the bodice near the top of the page; skirts need space
Pattern looks messySpace the dots or stripes evenly and keep them the same size

Drawing by age

  • Ages 3 to 4: a triangle skirt and a square top is a dress. Let them color it however they like; the grip practice is the point.
  • Ages 5 to 7: add sleeves, a neckline and one decoration. Ask what the dress is for, and who is wearing it where.
  • Ages 8 to 10: try the five styles, design a matching coat or bag, or draw the same dress in three color schemes and pick a favorite.

Turning drawings into play

A drawn dress can become a paper doll outfit. Draw it over a paper doll, add tabs at the shoulders and waist, and cut it out; the steps are in how to make paper dolls and clothes. Older children can keep a small "design book" with one dress per page and a name for each.

Questions

How do you draw a simple dress for kids?

Draw a small rectangle for the top, a narrow waist line under it, and a triangle or bell shape below for the skirt. Add a neckline, sleeves and a bow, then color.

How do you draw a frock easily?

A frock is a dress that ends around the knee. Draw a rectangle top and a straight triangle skirt that stops halfway down the legs, then add a round collar, buttons and a sash.

How do kids design their own dress?

Pick a skirt shape from the style table, choose two or three colors, and add one pattern and one detail such as a bow or frills. Giving the dress a purpose, such as a party or a snowy walk, helps children decide.

Doing this in Sewsie

In Sewsie, the garments are already drawn: children color each part with plain colors, blends, glitter or prints such as dots, stripes, checks and flowers, then sew the finished dress onto a doll and dress her up. It suits times when paper and pens are not handy; the steps on this page are for the rest of the time.
