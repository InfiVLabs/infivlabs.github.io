How often to water Ladyfinger cactus

In a 15 cm plastic pot in bright, indirect light, Ladyfinger cactus usually needs water about every 21 days while it grows and about every 40 days in midwinter (July) at the latitude of Sydney and Adelaide (further south, in Melbourne, Hobart and New Zealand, a little longer; in the tropical north, hardly any longer). Pot and spot change that:

PotSpring–summer (Sep–Feb)Autumn–winter (Mar–Aug)
12 cm, plastic18 days34 days
15 cm, terracotta17 days32 days
25 cm, plastic30 days57 days

These are starting points, not rules: push a finger or a wooden skewer deep into the soil before you water. Water only when it is dry all the way down; any dampness means wait.

Light: where to put Ladyfinger cactus

Best in direct light; it copes with bright, indirect. As a rough guide, measured at midday: low is under 1,100 lux, medium 1,100–5,400, bright indirect 5,400–10,800 and direct sun above that. A phone reading is approximate.

Is Ladyfinger cactus toxic to cats and dogs?

It is not on the ASPCA list, so Leaftag treats it as unsafe for pets as a precaution.

If a pet has eaten part of a plant, call your vet or the Animal Poisons Helpline: 1300 869 738 in Australia, 0800 869 738 in New Zealand (fees may apply). This page is general information, not veterinary advice.

Feeding and repotting

Feed about every 42 days from spring to early autumn (September to March), and less often through the cooler months. Use the fertiliser at a quarter of the strength on the label: its roots burn easily.

Humidity and temperature

Dry indoor air is fine. Comfortable between 5 °C and 35 °C.

Tips

  • Water thoroughly, then wait until the soil is completely dry.
  • A cool, dry winter rest encourages its ring of spring flowers.
