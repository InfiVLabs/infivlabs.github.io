How often to water Scarlet star

In a 15 cm plastic pot in bright, indirect light, Scarlet star usually needs water about every 7 days while it grows and about every 11 days in midwinter in northern Europe (nearer the equator, winter changes less). Pot and spot change that:

PotSpring–summerAutumn–winter
12 cm, plastic6 days10 days
15 cm, terracotta6 days9 days
25 cm, plastic10 days16 days

These are starting points, not rules: push a finger 3 cm into the soil before you water. Dry at that depth means water; still damp means wait.

Light: where to put Scarlet star

Best in bright, indirect light; it copes with medium. As a rough guide, measured at midday: low is under 1,100 lux, medium 1,100–5,400, bright indirect 5,400–10,800 and direct sun above that. A phone reading is approximate.

Is Scarlet star toxic to cats and dogs?

It is listed as non-toxic to cats and dogs.

If a pet has eaten part of a plant, call your vet or an animal poison line. This page is general information, not veterinary advice.

Sources: ASPCA: orange star

Feeding and repotting

Feed about every 30 days from spring to early autumn, less often as the days shorten, and not at all in midwinter. Use the fertiliser at a quarter of the strength on the label: its roots burn easily.

Humidity and temperature

Average room humidity suits it. Comfortable between 16 and 29 °C.

Tips

  • Keep a little water in the central cup; water the soil only when dry.
  • It flowers once, then makes pups; pot them up at a third of its size.

Other names

Guzmania · Bromeliad · Guzmanie · Bromelie · Bromelia · Broméliacée
