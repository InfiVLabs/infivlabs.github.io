How often to water Bird's nest fern

In a 15 cm plastic pot in bright, indirect light, Bird's nest fern usually needs water about every 5 days while it grows and about every 7 days in midwinter in the UK, Ireland and northern Europe (nearer the equator, winter changes less). Pot and spot change that:

PotSpring–summerAutumn–winter
12 cm, plastic4 days6 days
15 cm, terracotta4 days5 days
25 cm, plastic7 days9 days

These are starting points, not rules: push a finger 3 cm into the soil before you water. Dry at that depth means water; still damp means wait.

Light: where to put Bird's nest fern

Best in medium light; it copes with low. As a rough guide, measured at midday: low is under 1,100 lux, medium 1,100–5,400, bright indirect 5,400–10,800 and direct sun above that. A phone reading is approximate.

Is Bird's nest fern toxic to cats and dogs?

It is not on the ASPCA list, so Leaftag treats it as unsafe for pets as a precaution.

If a pet has eaten part of a plant, call your vet or, in the UK, Animal PoisonLine on 01202 509000 (a fee applies). This page is general information, not veterinary advice.

Feeding and repotting

Feed about every 28 days from spring to early autumn, less often as the days shorten, and not at all in midwinter. Use the fertiliser at a quarter of the strength on the label: its roots burn easily.

Humidity and temperature

It likes humid air: a humidifier helps most, grouping plants a little. Comfortable between 16 °C and 29 °C.

Tips

  • Water around the edge of the pot, not into the central rosette.
  • Water when the top 2 cm is dry; it tolerates lower light than most ferns.

Other names
