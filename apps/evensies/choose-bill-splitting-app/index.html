How to choose a bill splitting app

Choosing a bill splitting app for trips, roommates or a couple: nine things to check, from daily limits and sign-ups to split types, sync and price.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

The best bill splitting app is the one your group will actually use, so start with the people, not the features. Check whether everyone must create an account, whether the free version limits how many expenses you can add, whether it splits costs the way your group shares them, and how it shows who owes who at the end. Those four answers rule out most apps for most groups.

Below are the nine things worth checking, what to look for in each, and which kind of group each one matters to.

1. Does everyone need to sign up?

Some apps need every member to create an account before they can be added to a group. Others let one person keep the group and type in names. For a long-term flat share, accounts are fine. For a one-off trip with a friend's cousin you met on Friday, an app that needs no sign-up from the others is much easier.

Check: can you add a person by typing a name, and can they see the result without installing anything?

2. Are there limits on the free version?

This is where most frustration comes from. Free tiers are limited in different ways: a cap on how many expenses you can add per day, a cap on the number of groups, a time limit, or features held back such as currencies or receipts. A daily expense cap hurts most on trips, where a group can rack up twenty expenses in an evening.

Check: the limits page in the app store listing or the app's own pricing page. A limit on groups is easy to live with; a limit on entries is not.

3. Can it split costs the way you actually share them?

An equal split covers most dinners. Real life also needs:

  • Leaving people out of a cost they didn't share (the person who skipped the bar).
  • Exact amounts, for itemized restaurant bills.
  • Percentages, for rent split by income.
  • Shares, where the bigger room pays two shares to everyone else's one.
  • More than one payer, when two people split the hotel deposit at the desk.

Check: add one awkward real expense during the trial and see whether it takes ten seconds or a workaround.

4. How does it show who owes who?

At the end, you want a short list of payments, not a web of small debts. Good apps simplify debts: they replace every pair-by-pair debt with the fewest payments that leave everyone even. For four people, that is at most three payments. If you want to see how that math works, the four-friend trip example walks through it.

Check: can you tap a balance and see how it was calculated? An explanation stops most arguments before they start.

5. Does the group stay in sync?

Here apps differ the most, and it matters more than any feature list.

  • Cloud-synced apps let everyone add expenses from their own phone and see changes instantly. They need accounts and an internet connection, and your group's data lives on the company's servers.
  • Single-device or file-based apps keep the group on one phone. Others either receive the summary, or get a copy to add to and send back for merging. They work offline and keep data private, but changes aren't instant.

Check: if four people will each add costs during the trip from different places, live sync is worth a lot. If one person handles the money, it isn't.

6. Where is your data, and what does the app need?

A cost tracker knows where you went, what you spent and with whom. Look at what it asks for: contacts access, location, a phone number, a link to your bank. None of those are needed to split a bill.

Check: the app's privacy section in the store listing, and whether it works with no account at all.

7. Does it handle other currencies?

For trips abroad, look for expenses in a foreign currency with an exchange rate you can set, so everyone is charged at the rate the group agreed rather than a rate that moves after the trip.

8. Can you get your data out?

A spreadsheet (CSV) or PDF export matters for flat shares that run for years, for work trips that need receipts, and for leaving the app later without losing your history.

9. How is it paid for?

Check whether the paid version is a subscription or a one-time payment, and what the ads are like in the free version. A monthly subscription for something you use twice a year adds up. Ads that pop up while you are typing in an expense are worse than a small banner.

Also check whether the app moves money. Some splitting apps include payments or a card; most, including the one below, are trackers only: they tell you who pays whom and you pay each other the usual way.

Questions

What is the best app to split expenses with friends?

There is no single best one; it depends on your group. For a trip where one person keeps track, pick an app with no sign-up for the others and no daily limit. For a group where everyone adds costs as they go, pick one with live sync.

Is there a bill splitting app with no daily limit?

Yes. Evensies, for one, puts no cap on the number of expenses any user can add, free or paid. Whatever app you try, check the free tier's limits in the store listing before you start a trip.

Do bill splitting apps send money?

Most don't. They track costs and tell you who owes whom; you pay with cash, a bank transfer or a payment app. A few include payments or a card. Read the listing to know which kind you have.

Can I split bills without an app?

Yes. List every cost with who paid and who shared it, work out each person's balance (paid minus share), then have those who owe pay those who are owed. A spreadsheet works for small groups; an app saves time when there are many expenses.
