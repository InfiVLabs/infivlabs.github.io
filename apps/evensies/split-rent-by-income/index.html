How to split rent based on income

How to split rent based on income: the one-line formula, worked examples for a couple and three roommates, and when an income split stops being fair.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

To split rent based on income, give each person the same percentage of the rent as their share of the household's total income. The formula is: your rent = total rent × your income ÷ everyone's income added together. If you earn 60% of the combined income, you pay 60% of the rent. Everyone then spends the same proportion of what they earn on housing.

How to split rent by income: the formula

  1. Agree which income you are using (more on that below) and write down each person's monthly figure.
  2. Add the incomes together.
  3. Divide each person's income by the total. That is their percentage.
  4. Multiply the rent by each percentage.
  5. Check that the amounts add up to the rent exactly. Fix any rounding cent by giving it to one person.

Example 1: a couple splitting rent by income

Maya and Leo rent a $2,400 apartment. Maya takes home $6,000 a month and Leo $4,000.

Three roommates share a $3,000 place. Priya earns $5,000 a month, Sam $3,500 and Jo $1,500, so the household total is $10,000.

Between friends who chose to live together, that difference is worth talking about openly. Between roommates who found each other through a listing, most people expect equal rent or a split by room, because the income split asks the higher earner to cover part of someone else's housing.

Gross or take-home pay?

Use take-home pay (after tax) if you can. It is what each person can actually spend, and two people with the same salary can take home very different amounts after tax, retirement savings or student loan payments. Gross pay is simpler and easier to verify. Whichever you choose, use the same measure for everyone.

For irregular income (freelance work, commission, tips), use an average of the last three to six months and revisit it on a fixed date, such as every January or at lease renewal.

Three ways to split by income

The straight percentage above is the common version, but couples in particular use a few variations.

  • Percentage of income (the formula above). Each person spends the same share of income on rent. Simple, easy to explain, and it updates itself when someone's pay changes.
  • Equal leftover money. Rent is set so both people keep the same amount after paying it. With Maya and Leo: combined income $10,000, minus rent $2,400, leaves $7,600, or $3,800 each. Maya pays $6,000 − $3,800 = $2,200 and Leo pays $200. This only makes sense for couples who already pool most of their finances; between roommates it is rarely seen as fair.
  • A floor, then income. Everyone pays a fixed minimum (for example, $600 each), and the rest is split by income. It keeps the lower earner contributing a meaningful amount while still easing the load.

When an income split stops being fair

  • One person has much more space. If the higher earner also gets the big room with its own bathroom, combine methods: split part of the rent by room size and adjust the rest by income.
  • Debts or dependents. A person paying child support or caring for a parent may have a high income but little to spare. Take-home pay after fixed obligations is fairer here.
  • Big swings in income. If someone's pay drops, an income split protects them; if it rises, they pay more. Agree in advance how often you recalculate.
  • Other shared costs. Decide whether utilities, groceries and internet follow the same percentages or are split equally. Many couples use income for rent and equal shares for everything else.

What to agree on before you start

Write down, in a shared note or message, which income figure you used, the resulting percentages, which bills they cover, and when you will review them. Five minutes of writing prevents months of quiet resentment.

Splitting rent by income in Evensies

In Evensies, add the rent once and choose the percent split: type 60 and 40 (or 50, 35 and 15) and it shows when the percentages add up to 100. Set the expense to repeat every month, and each month's rent lands in everyone's balance. Utilities can sit in the same group with an equal split. Questions

Is it fair to split rent based on income?

It is fair when everyone agrees it is. Couples often prefer it because each person spends the same share of their pay on housing. Roommates more often split equally or by room size, since an income split asks one person to cover part of another's housing.

How do you calculate rent by income percentage?

Divide your income by the household's combined income to get your percentage, then multiply the rent by it. Earning $4,000 of a combined $10,000 means paying 40% of the rent.

Should couples split bills 50/50 or by income?

If your incomes are close, 50/50 is simpler and works fine. If one person earns much more, an income split keeps housing from taking a far bigger bite out of one person's pay. Many couples split rent by income and everything else equally.

Do you use gross or net income to split rent?

Net (take-home) income is usually fairer, because it is the money each person actually has. Gross is simpler to check. Pick one and use it for everyone.
