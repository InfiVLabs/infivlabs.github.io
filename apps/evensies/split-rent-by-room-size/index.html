How to split rent by room size

Split rent by room size the fair way: shared space split equally, bedrooms by square footage, with worked numbers, a couple-in-one-room case and extras.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

To split rent by room size, divide the rent into two parts: the shared space (kitchen, living room, hallways) that everyone uses equally, and the bedrooms, which are paid in proportion to their floor area. Each person pays an equal slice of the shared part plus their bedroom's share of the private part. This works the same in square feet or square meters.

Splitting the whole rent by bedroom size alone is simpler, but it overcharges the big room, because everyone uses the kitchen and bathroom the same amount whatever size their bedroom is.

Step 1: measure the bedrooms

Measure each bedroom's length and width and multiply. Include built-in closets that belong to the room. Leave out shared hallways and bathrooms. Rough measurements are fine as long as everyone measures the same way.

Our example: a three-bedroom apartment for $3,000 a month.

RoomSizeShare of bedroom area
Room A180 sq ft180 ÷ 440 = 40.9%
Room B140 sq ft140 ÷ 440 = 31.8%
Room C120 sq ft120 ÷ 440 = 27.3%
Total440 sq ft100%

Step 2: decide how much of the rent is for shared space

This is the judgment call. A common starting point is to put half the rent toward shared space and half toward the bedrooms. If the bedrooms are small and the living area is large, move it toward 60% shared; if the bedrooms are most of the apartment, toward 30% shared. You can estimate it from the floor plan: the share of the apartment's total area that is not bedrooms.

Step 3: calculate each person's rent

With a 50/50 split of the $3,000 rent:

  • Shared part: $1,500, split equally = $500 each
  • Bedroom part: $1,500, split by area

Adjusting for features that aren't size

Size is only one thing that makes a room worth more. Agree on a fixed monthly amount for each extra and move it from the other rooms to the room that has it:

  • Private bathroom: often the biggest premium. For example, $100 more for that room, taken $50 off each of the other two.
  • Balcony, walk-in closet or better light: a smaller premium, such as $25 to $50.
  • Parking space: charge it to whoever uses it, at what a local space would cost.
  • Noise or a walk-through room: a discount for the room next to the kitchen or the one others pass through.

Keep every adjustment a round number the group agrees on. Trying to price everything exactly turns a five-minute conversation into a long one.

How to split rent between a couple and a single

When a couple shares one bedroom, they use the shared space twice as much as a single person: two people cooking, showering and sitting on the sofa. So count people for the shared part and rooms for the bedroom part.

Common mistakes

  • Splitting purely by bedroom size. The biggest room ends up paying for a kitchen it uses no more than anyone else.
  • Skipping the conversation about extras. The private bathroom is the usual source of quiet resentment months later.
  • Rounding each person's rent separately. Round to the cent and check the total equals the rent exactly; give any leftover cent to one person.
  • Never revisiting it. If someone moves rooms, a couple moves in, or the rent goes up, run the numbers again.

Questions

What is the fairest way to split rent with roommates?

For most shared homes, split the shared space equally and the bedrooms by size, then agree on small fixed adjustments for extras like a private bathroom. It reflects how the space is used and is easy to explain.

How do you split rent by square footage?

Divide each bedroom's area by the total bedroom area to get its percentage, then apply that percentage to the part of the rent you assign to bedrooms. Split the rest equally among everyone.

How should rent be split between a couple and a single person?

Split the shared-space part per person, so the couple pays two slices, and the bedroom part per room. The couple pays more in total than a single person, but less each.

Does this work in square meters?

Yes. The method only uses proportions, so square feet and square meters give exactly the same rent.
