Habit tracker printable: a weekly grid, a monthly page and a spreadsheet

A habit tracker printable you can copy: a weekly grid, a monthly layout for squared paper, and a Google Sheets or Excel template with formulas.

Updated 26 Nov 20264 min read

A printable habit tracker is a grid with your habits as rows and the days as columns: you fill a square each time you do the habit. Below is a weekly template you can print from this page, the exact layout for a monthly tracker on graph paper, and a spreadsheet version for Google Sheets or Excel with the formulas written out.

Weekly habit tracker template

Print this page or copy the table into a notebook. Write up to six habits, one per row, and fill or cross a square each day. The last column is for the week's count.

HabitMonTueWedThuFriSatSunDone
Example: read 10 pagesxxxxx5/7

Tips for the weekly page:

  • Write the rule for "done" next to the habit name, for example "walk 20 min (5 on bad days)".
  • For a "3 times a week" habit, write 3 in the Done column as the target, so the empty days do not look like failures.
  • Shade planned rest days diagonally before the week starts. They are part of the plan, not misses.

Monthly habit tracker on squared paper

A monthly tracker needs 31 day columns plus room for the habit names. Squared paper makes this easy without a printer:

  • A4 with 5 mm squares: the sheet is 210 mm wide, or 42 squares. Use 8 squares for names and 31 for the days, leaving a narrow margin. Portrait works.
  • US Letter with quarter-inch squares: the sheet is 34 squares wide in portrait, too narrow for 31 days plus names. Turn it to landscape (44 squares) and keep 10 squares for names.
  • Rows: give each habit two squares of height so there is room to fill them neatly, and leave a blank row between groups (health, study, home).
  • Header: write the day numbers across the top and mark weekends with a dot so you can see the weekly rhythm.

At the end of the month, count the filled squares per row and write the total in the margin. Comparing month to month tells you more than a streak does.

Google Sheets and Excel habit tracker template

A spreadsheet tracker counts for you. Set it up once and copy the sheet each month.

  1. Row 1: in A1 type "Habit", then the day numbers 1 to 31 in B1 to AF1. In AG1 type "Done".
  2. Rows 2 to 7: one habit per row in column A.
  3. Checkboxes (Google Sheets): select B2:AF7 and choose Insert, then Checkbox. Each cell becomes a tick box that holds TRUE or FALSE.
  4. Count (Google Sheets): in AG2 enter =COUNTIF(B2:AF2,TRUE) and fill it down to AG7.
  5. Excel version: instead of checkboxes, type an x in a cell when you do the habit, and in AG2 enter =COUNTIF(B2:AF2,"x").
  6. Color the done cells: use conditional formatting on B2:AF7. In Google Sheets, choose "Custom formula is" with =B2=TRUE; in Excel, choose "Format only cells that contain", Cell Value, equal to x. Pick a fill color.
  7. Percentage for the month: in AH2 enter =AG2/31 (or the number of days in that month) and format it as a percentage.

To keep a year in one file, copy the sheet twelve times and name each tab after its month.

Common mistakes with printable trackers

  • Too many rows. Twelve habits on day one makes a page mostly empty by week two.
  • Filling squares from memory at the end of the week. Fill them on the day, right after the habit.
  • Starting over after a bad week. Keep the page, turn it and carry on. A missed day does little harm to a habit that is forming, as the research in how long it takes to form a habit shows.

Questions

How do I make a habit tracker in Google Sheets?

Put habits in column A and the days 1 to 31 across row 1, turn the day cells into checkboxes with Insert, then Checkbox, and count each row with =COUNTIF(B2:AF2,TRUE).

What size squares are best for a monthly habit tracker?

On A4, 5 mm squares fit 31 days plus a name column in portrait. On US Letter with quarter-inch squares, turn the page to landscape.

Should I use a weekly or a monthly habit tracker?

A weekly page is easier to start and leaves room for notes. A monthly page shows patterns better, such as which weekday you usually skip.
