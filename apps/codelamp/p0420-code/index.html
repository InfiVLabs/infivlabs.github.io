P0420 code: catalytic converter efficiency below threshold (bank 1)

P0420 means the catalytic converter on bank 1 is not cleaning the exhaust well enough. Whether you can drive, the likely causes and what to check first.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

P0420 means the car's computer thinks the catalytic converter on bank 1 is no longer cleaning the exhaust as well as it should. The standard wording is "Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold (Bank 1)". You can usually keep driving, but fix the cause within the next few weeks: the car will fail an emissions test, and the problem that wore the converter out can keep damaging it.

This page is general information, not a professional diagnosis. If the check engine light is flashing, stop as soon as it is safe; that is a misfire, and it needs attention first.

What P0420 means

Your car has an oxygen sensor before the catalytic converter and one after it. The front sensor's signal swings up and down quickly as the engine adjusts the fuel mixture. A healthy converter stores and uses oxygen, so the rear sensor's signal stays fairly steady. When the converter wears out, it stops smoothing things out, and the rear sensor starts to swing like the front one. Once the two readings look too much alike, the computer sets P0420.

So P0420 is a conclusion drawn from two sensors. It does not prove the converter itself has failed. A sensor that reads wrong, or an exhaust leak that lets outside air reach a sensor, can produce the same result.

Bank 1 is the side of the engine with cylinder 1. On a four-cylinder engine there is only one bank. On a V6 or V8, the same fault on the other side sets P0430 (bank 2). If you have both, look for a cause the two sides share, such as a misfire or a fuel mixture problem.

Can you drive with a P0420 code?

Usually yes. P0420 is an emissions fault, not a safety one, and the car normally drives as before. Two cautions:

  • If the converter is badly blocked, which is less common, the car can feel weak when accelerating or on hills, and the exhaust can get very hot. That needs a shop soon.
  • If P0420 appears with a misfire code (P0300 to P0308), the misfire comes first. Unburned fuel from a misfire overheats the converter, so driving on with it can turn a sensor-level fix into a converter replacement. Read what P0300 means.

P0420 symptoms

Often there are none besides the light. When there are, they include:

  • A failed emissions or smog test, even if the car feels normal.
  • A sulfur or rotten-egg smell from the exhaust.
  • Sluggish acceleration, if the converter is partly blocked.
  • A rattle from under the car, if the converter's insides have broken up.

Likely causes, cheapest checks first

  1. An exhaust leak near the oxygen sensors. A cracked manifold, a loose joint or a failed gasket lets air in and confuses the sensors. A ticking sound when the engine is cold is a clue.
  2. A lazy rear oxygen sensor. Sensors age. One that responds slowly or reads wrongly can trigger P0420 on a converter that is still fine.
  3. Misfires or rich running. Worn spark plugs, a failing coil or a leaking injector send unburned fuel into the converter and wear it out. Fix these first, or a new converter will go the same way.
  4. A worn-out catalytic converter. After years and many miles, the converter's coating loses its ability to clean the exhaust. This is the most expensive cause, so rule out the others before replacing it.

What to do, step by step

  1. Read all the codes, not just P0420. Any misfire, fuel mixture (P0171, P0174) or oxygen sensor code is the better place to start.
  2. Have those fixed first, then clear the codes and drive normally for a few days.
  3. Watch whether P0420 returns. If it does not come back after several drives, the earlier problem was the cause.
  4. If it returns, have a shop check for exhaust leaks and test the rear oxygen sensor before agreeing to a new converter.
  5. Check the warranty. In the US, federal law covers the catalytic converter for 8 years or 80,000 miles, whichever comes first, as a specified major emission control component. If your car is within that period, ask the dealer whether the repair is covered before paying for it.

Clearing P0420 before an emissions test

Clearing the code turns the light off, but it also resets the readiness monitors, the self-tests the inspection checks. US guidelines allow only one unfinished monitor on 2001 and newer cars, and the catalyst monitor needs a stretch of steady driving to finish. If the converter is still weak, the monitor will find it again during that driving and the light will come back. Clearing just before an inspection usually turns a fail into a "not ready" result instead of a pass.

Questions

Is P0420 serious?

Not urgent in most cases. It is an emissions fault and the car usually drives normally. It becomes serious if a misfire is causing it, or if the converter is blocked and the car feels weak. Get it checked within a few weeks.

Can a P0420 code clear itself?

Yes, if the cause goes away. After the fault stops, the light usually turns off by itself once the catalyst self-test passes on a few drives. If the converter is truly worn, the code comes back.

Can a bad oxygen sensor cause P0420?

Yes. The code is set by comparing the oxygen sensors before and after the converter, so a slow or faulty rear sensor can set P0420 even when the converter works. Testing the sensor costs far less than replacing a converter.

What is the difference between P0420 and P0430?

They mean the same thing on different sides of a V-shaped engine. P0420 is bank 1, the side with cylinder 1. P0430 is bank 2.
