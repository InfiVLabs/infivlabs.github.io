P0300 code: random or multiple cylinder misfire detected

P0300 means misfires in several cylinders at random. Can you drive with it, what a flashing light changes, the common causes and what to check first.

Updated 26 Nov 20264 min read

P0300 means the engine computer has detected misfires in more than one cylinder, or cannot tell which cylinder is misfiring. You may feel the engine shake at idle, stumble or pull weakly. Drive gently and get it checked within days. If the check engine light starts flashing, pull over safely and stop: the misfire is now severe enough to damage the catalytic converter.

This page is general information, not a professional diagnosis. Stop driving if the engine shakes hard, loses power, or the light flashes.

What a misfire is

Each cylinder should burn its fuel on every power stroke. A misfire is a stroke where that does not happen, because the spark, the fuel or the air is wrong. The computer notices it as a tiny slowdown in the crankshaft's rotation. Count enough of them and it sets a code.

The P03xx misfire codes come in two kinds:

CodeMeaning
P0300Random or multiple cylinders misfiring
P0301 to P0308One cylinder misfiring; the last digit is the cylinder number (P0304 is cylinder 4)

The difference matters. A single-cylinder code points at that cylinder's own parts: its spark plug, coil or injector. P0300 points at something all cylinders share: the fuel supply, the air going in, or the ignition parts as a whole. P0300 together with one or two cylinder codes usually means those cylinders are the worst of a general problem.

Can you drive with a P0300 code?

What you noticeWhat to do
Light steady, car feels almost normalDrive gently, short trips only, and book a check within days.
Light steady, noticeable shaking or hesitationDrive only as far as a shop. Avoid highways, towing and hard acceleration.
Light flashingSlow down, stop where it is safe and switch off. Arrange a tow if it keeps flashing after a restart.

The reason for the caution is the catalytic converter. Unburned fuel from a misfire goes into the exhaust and burns inside the converter, which can overheat it. A misfire left alone is one of the most common reasons a car later sets P0420, the catalytic converter code. For more on the flashing light, see check engine light flashing or steady.

Common causes of P0300, cheapest checks first

  1. Worn spark plugs. Plugs past their service interval need more voltage to spark and start to miss under load. Check the mileage in the service book.
  2. A vacuum leak from a cracked hose. Extra air the computer cannot measure leans out the mixture in every cylinder. A hissing sound at idle is a clue. A lean code such as P0171 often appears with it.
  3. A dirty or failing mass airflow (MAF) sensor. If it under-reports the air coming in, every cylinder gets too little fuel.
  4. Low fuel pressure or poor fuel. A weak fuel pump, a clogged filter or a bad tank of fuel affects all cylinders together.
  5. Failing ignition coils or plug wires. One weak coil sets a single-cylinder code; several aging coils or wires can set P0300.

Less common: a fault in the crankshaft or camshaft sensor, a slipped timing chain or belt, or low compression. These need a shop.

What to check, step by step

  1. Read every code, including pending ones. Single-cylinder codes and lean codes narrow the search fast.
  2. Note when it misfires. Cold start only, at idle, under load going uphill, or in rain? Misfires that get worse in wet weather point at plug wires or coils.
  3. Look at the basics. When were the spark plugs last changed? Any cracked or disconnected hoses on top of the engine? Did it start right after filling up?
  4. Take your notes to a mechanic. With a single-cylinder code alongside P0300, a common shop test is to move that cylinder's coil to another cylinder: if the misfire follows the coil, the coil is at fault; if it stays, the plug or injector is next.
  5. If the engine shakes badly, or the light flashes, stop there and have it diagnosed. Continuing to drive risks the catalytic converter.

Clearing P0300

Clearing the code without a repair only hides it. The computer will count the misfires again and the light will return, sometimes within one drive. Clearing also resets the emissions self-tests. Clear it after a repair, then watch whether it comes back.

Questions

Is P0300 serious?

It can be. A light misfire is not an emergency, but it gets worse over time and can damage the catalytic converter. If the light is flashing or the engine shakes hard, stop driving.

Can bad gas cause a P0300 code?

Yes. Contaminated fuel or fuel with water in it can make every cylinder misfire. If the problem started right after filling up, that is worth mentioning to your mechanic.

Will new spark plugs fix P0300?

Only if worn plugs are the cause. They are the cheapest thing to check, which is why they come first, but a vacuum leak, an airflow sensor or low fuel pressure produce the same code.

What is the difference between P0300 and P0301?

P0300 means misfires in several cylinders or ones the computer cannot pin down. P0301 means cylinder 1 alone is misfiring, which points at that cylinder's own plug, coil or injector.
