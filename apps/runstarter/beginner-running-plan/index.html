Beginner running plan: 9 weeks from walking to running 5K

A 9-week beginner running plan: three walk-run sessions a week, from 1-minute runs to 30 minutes nonstop, with the full week-by-week table.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

This beginner running plan takes you from running one minute at a time to running 30 minutes without stopping in nine weeks. You run three times a week on non-consecutive days. Every session starts with a five-minute warm-up walk and ends with a five-minute cool-down walk, and the running in between grows a little each week. Thirty minutes of easy running is about 5K for many people.

The biggest jumps are week 4 (the first five-minute runs) and the last run of week 6 (the first run with no walking at all). Both are meant to feel like a stretch. If one feels too much, repeat the previous session and try again.

How fast should you run?

Slowly. Every running interval should be at a pace where you could still speak in sentences. If you can only get out a few words, slow down; if you still can't, walk until you can. This "talk test" is a simple way to keep your effort moderate, and it is how most beginners make it through week 4.

Pace does not matter in this plan. Time on your feet does. A slow jog that you can keep going beats a fast run that stops after two minutes.

Three runs a week: how to schedule them

Put a rest day between runs, for example Monday, Wednesday and Saturday. Rest days are when your legs adapt, so do not squeeze two sessions into one day to catch up. A walk, cycling or stretching on a rest day is fine.

If three a week does not fit your life, two runs a week works too. Do the same sessions in the same order; the plan takes about 14 weeks instead of 9.

Can you get ready for a 5K in 4 or 6 weeks?

From no running at all, four weeks is tight. You can finish a 5K by walking part of it, and that is a real finish. Running it the whole way usually takes longer, because your legs usually need more time to get used to running than your breathing does.

If you already run 10 to 15 minutes comfortably, start at week 4 or 5 and you can be ready in about five or six weeks. If you are not sure, start at week 1: the first sessions feel easy for a fit beginner and that is fine.

Is 30 minutes the same as 5K?

Not always. At 6 minutes per kilometer (about 9:39 per mile), 30 minutes is exactly 5K. Many beginners run slower than that and cover 4 to 4.5 km. Both are good. Once you can run 30 minutes, adding the last few minutes to reach 5K is a small step. Our page on average 5K times has a pace table.

On a treadmill

The same plan works indoors. Set the running speed where you pass the talk test; for many beginners that is a slow jog, only a little faster than a brisk walk. Walk the breaks at an easy pace, and change the speed with the buttons when the interval changes rather than stepping off the belt. Increase the speed only when a whole session feels easy.

When to repeat a week

  • You could not finish the running parts without stopping, even when slowing down.
  • You are still tired from the last session when the next one is due.
  • You missed more than a week. Go back one week and carry on from there.

Repeating a week is part of the plan, not a failure. Plenty of people take longer than nine weeks.

This plan is for healthy adults. If you are new to exercise, or have a heart condition, joint problems, chest pain, dizziness or another health condition, check with a doctor first. Stop and walk if you feel pain, dizziness or chest discomfort.

If you are starting from very little exercise, read how to start running when you're out of shape before week 1. If you are starting in January, the New Year running plan puts these nine weeks on the 2027 calendar.

Questions

What is the best running plan for beginners?

One that starts with short runs and walking breaks, adds a little each week, and keeps every run slow enough to talk. Three sessions a week for about nine weeks suits most beginners.

How long should a beginner run each time?

In week 1, about 8 minutes of running inside a 30-minute session. By week 9 the session is 30 minutes of running plus a warm-up and cool-down walk.

What if I can't finish a session?

Walk, then repeat the same session next time. If a whole week feels too hard, repeat the week. Moving on only when the current step feels manageable is what gets people to the end.

How many days a week should a beginner run?

Three days a week with a rest day between runs is a good rhythm. Two days a week also works if the plan is stretched over more weeks.
