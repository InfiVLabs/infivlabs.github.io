How to tell when and where a photo was taken

How to check when and where a photo was taken on iPhone, Android, Windows and Mac, how far to trust the date, and what makes a timestamp convincing.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

To find out when a photo was taken, open its information: on iPhone, swipe up on the photo in Photos; in Google Photos, swipe up; on a computer, look at the file's properties. That shows the date, time and often the location the camera saved. But that stored information can be edited, removed by chat apps, or wrong from the start if the phone's clock was wrong, so it tells you when a photo was probably taken, not when it certainly was.

How to check when a photo was taken

Where the photo isHow to see the date, time and place
iPhone, in PhotosOpen the photo, then swipe up or tap the Info button.
Android, in Google PhotosOpen the photo and swipe up for its details.
WindowsRight-click the file, choose Properties, then the Details tab. Look for "Date taken" and the GPS section.
MacOpen the file in Preview, choose Tools, then Show Inspector, and look at the EXIF and GPS tabs.

Look at "date taken", not "date modified" or "date created". The file's created date changes when the photo is copied, downloaded or saved from a chat, so it often shows the day you received the photo.

How far you can trust the date

The date and place stored in a photo come with three weaknesses:

  1. They can be changed. Apple's own Photos app has Adjust Date & Time and Adjust Location for any photo, and plenty of other apps can do the same. Nothing marks the change in the picture.
  2. They are often missing. Many chat apps remove the metadata when a picture is sent as a normal photo, and screenshots have only the time of the screenshot.
  3. They depend on the phone. If the clock was set wrong, or location was off, the stored facts are wrong or empty, and the photo itself gives no warning.

So a date in the metadata is a good clue for your own photos and a weak one for a photo someone else sent you.

What is a timestamped photo?

A timestamped photo has the date and time printed on the picture itself, usually in a corner. Some cameras and apps also print the location and address. Because the stamp is part of the image, it survives being sent in chat apps, printed or put in a report, and anyone can read it without opening the photo's information.

A stamp is still made of pixels, though. Anyone with a photo editor can paint over it or add one to an old photo. A stamp is most convincing when:

  • It was made by the camera at the moment of capture, not added later.
  • It includes details that are hard to guess afterward, such as the seconds, the time zone, GPS coordinates with their accuracy, and the address.
  • There is a way to check whether the photo changed after it was taken.

How to check if a photo has been edited

No method is certain, but these catch most changes:

  • Ask for the original file, not a copy from a chat or a screenshot. Compare it with the copy you received.
  • Look at the metadata for an editing app named in the software field, or a "date modified" long after "date taken".
  • Look closely at the stamp. Uneven fonts, blurred edges around the text, or a stamp that does not match the light of the picture are signs it was added.
  • Search for earlier copies with a reverse image search. If the same photo appears online before its stamped date, the stamp is wrong.
  • Use the app's own check, if the photo came from a camera that offers one.

Does a timestamped photo count as proof?

It depends on who is deciding. Insurers, landlords, clients and employers each set their own rules for the photos they accept, so ask them. In court, a timestamp is evidence, not a verdict. In US federal courts, for example, whoever offers a photo must show it is what they claim it is, and that is commonly done by a person who can say it fairly shows what they saw. A stamp made at capture, with its location and a way to show the photo has not changed since, supports that. It does not replace it.

This is general information, not legal advice. If a photo matters to a claim or a dispute, talk to a lawyer about what your court or insurer requires.

Whatever the use, these habits help:

  • Keep the original files, not only the copies you sent.
  • Keep the phone's date, time and time zone on automatic.
  • Take a wide shot first that shows where you are, then the close-ups.
  • Write down who took the photos and when, while you remember.

Questions

How can I tell when a photo was taken on iPhone?

Open the photo in Photos and swipe up or tap the Info button. You will see the date, time, the device that took it and, if location was on, a map. Keep in mind that these details can be adjusted in the same app.

Does the iPhone time stamp photos?

It records the date and time in each photo's information, but it does not print them on the picture. For a visible stamp you need a timestamp camera app.

Can the date of a photo be changed?

Yes. The date stored in a photo can be changed in many gallery apps, including iPhone Photos. A date printed on the photo at capture is harder to change without leaving visible traces.

Are timestamped photos admissible as evidence?

That is for the court to decide, case by case. A timestamp can support a photo, but courts usually also want someone who can say what the photo shows and when it was taken. Ask a lawyer about your situation.
