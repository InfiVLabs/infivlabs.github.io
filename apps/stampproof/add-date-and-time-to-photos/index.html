How to add date and time to photos on iPhone and Android

How to add date and time to photos on iPhone, Samsung, Pixel and other Android phones, why most cameras hide it, and what to do for old photos.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

Every phone already records the date and time of each photo, but most keep it hidden in the file's metadata instead of printing it on the picture. To get a visible date and time stamp, you need a camera setting that burns it in (some Samsung and Xiaomi phones have one) or a timestamp camera app (iPhone and Pixel have no built-in option). For a photo you already took, you can only add the date as text afterward, which is not the same thing.

Visible stamp vs hidden metadata

There are two places a date can live:

Hidden metadata (EXIF)Burned-in stamp
Where it isInside the file, read by appsPrinted on the pixels of the photo
Who sees itOnly someone who opens the photo's infoAnyone who looks at the picture
Survives a chat appOften removed when sent as a normal photoYes, it is part of the image
Can be changedYes, in many gallery appsNot without visibly editing the picture

If you want someone else to see when a photo was taken, say a client, a landlord or an insurance adjuster, you want the burned-in stamp. If you only want to sort your own pictures by date, the metadata already does that.

How to add date and time to photos on Samsung

Many Samsung Galaxy phones have a watermark setting in the camera:

  1. Open the Camera app and tap the settings gear.
  2. Tap Watermark and turn it on.
  3. Turn on Date and time. You can also change the text, font and alignment.

Samsung's own help page notes two limits: the watermark appears only on photos taken after you turn it on, and it cannot be removed afterward. Some older Galaxy models do not have the setting.

Xiaomi, Redmi and other Android phones

Check your camera's settings for Watermark, Timestamp or Date stamp. Some Xiaomi and Redmi models offer a watermark with the time, and on some it is switched off by default. The name and options vary by model and software version, so if you find nothing, your camera does not have it.

How to timestamp photos on iPhone

The iPhone Camera app has no setting to print the date on a photo. It saves the date, time and, if allowed, the location in the photo's metadata. To see it, open the photo in Photos and swipe up or tap the Info button.

For a visible stamp on an iPhone photo, use a timestamp camera app from the App Store. Look for one that stamps at the moment of capture, not afterward.

How to add date and time to photos on Pixel

Google's Pixel Camera also has no date-stamp setting. The date and time are saved in the metadata; in Google Photos, open the photo and swipe up to see them. For a stamp on the image, use a timestamp camera app from Google Play.

Choosing a timestamp camera app

Search for "timestamp camera" and you will find dozens. Before you rely on one for work, check:

  • It stamps when you press the shutter. An app that lets you type any date on any photo cannot show when a photo was taken.
  • The text is readable on a phone screen. Tiny stamps disappear once the photo is shared in a chat.
  • It shows the time zone, if you work across zones or send photos to someone elsewhere.
  • It adds what you need: location, address, a job name or a note.
  • What the free plan includes, and whether it adds its own mark to the photo.

How to add date and time to photos already taken

No camera or app can truthfully stamp an old photo with its capture time after the fact. What you can do:

  1. Read the original date from the photo's info (Info on iPhone, swipe up in Google Photos).
  2. Type it on as text with a photo editor, such as the Markup tool in iPhone Photos or the text tool in any Android editor.

That is fine for a family album. For anything where the date matters to someone else, be open that the text was added later. iPhone Photos, like many gallery apps, lets anyone change a photo's stored date and time, and a typed date proves nothing on its own. For more on this, read how to tell when a photo was taken.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong phone clock. A stamp is only as right as the clock behind it. Leave the date and time on automatic.
  • Location turned off for the camera. On iPhone, check Settings, Privacy & Security, Location Services, Camera. On Android, check the camera app's location setting.
  • Sending through a chat app as a normal photo. The metadata is usually stripped, though a burned-in stamp stays.
  • Stamp in the wrong corner, where it sits on top of the thing you are photographing.

Questions

Does the iPhone camera have a timestamp option?

No. The iPhone saves the date and time in each photo's information, which you can see by swiping up on the photo in Photos, but it does not print them on the picture. For a visible stamp you need a timestamp camera app.

How do I put the date and time on photos on my Samsung phone?

Open the Camera app, tap settings, then Watermark, and turn on Date and time. It applies to photos you take after turning it on. Some older models do not have the option.

Can I add date and time to photos already taken?

You can type the date onto the picture with a photo editor, using the date in the photo's information. It is text added afterward, not a capture stamp, so it should not be presented as one.

Why did the date disappear when I sent the photo?

Many chat apps remove a photo's metadata when you send it as a normal photo. Only a date printed on the image itself survives.
