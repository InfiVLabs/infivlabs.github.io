Dry January 2027: dates, plan and tips for 31 days alcohol-free

Dry January 2027 runs Friday, January 1 to Sunday, January 31. A week-by-week plan, tips for five weekends, and who should see a doctor first.

Updated 26 Nov 20266 min read

Dry January 2027 starts on Friday, January 1 and ends on Sunday, January 31: 31 days without alcohol. The challenge is run by the UK charity Alcohol Change UK, and people everywhere now use the name for a month off drinking. Your last drink, if you have one, is on New Year's Eve.

Read this first: if you drink heavily most days, or get shaky, sweaty or anxious when you go without, do not stop all at once. Alcohol Change UK warns that people who are clinically dependent on alcohol can die if they suddenly stop completely. Talk to a doctor before January 1. In the US, the SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-4357) is free, confidential and open 24/7; in the UK, call Drinkline on 0300 123 1110.

January 2027 is a long one for drinkers: it starts on a Friday and ends on a Sunday, so it holds five Friday nights and five full weekends. Plan for those first.

Before January 1: set it up in December

  1. Decide what "dry" means for you. Most people mean no alcohol at all for 31 days. If you'd rather cut down, some people call that "damp January"; be clear with yourself which one you are doing.
  2. Clear the house, or most of it. The NIAAA's Rethinking Drinking advice for handling urges starts with keeping little or no alcohol at home.
  3. Buy your replacements now. Alcohol-free beer, a good tonic, sparkling water, a tea you like. Having something cold in a nice glass at 6 pm can matter more than you would expect.
  4. Write down three reasons. Sleep, money, a health check you have coming up, a person you want to show up for. Keep them on your phone.
  5. Tell one or two people. A friend who knows will order you a soda without asking why.
  6. Check your calendar. Note every birthday, work happy hour and game day in January and decide now what you'll drink and when you'll leave.

A week-by-week plan for Dry January

Week 1: January 1-7

The first weekend arrives on day two. New Year's Day may feel easy if you are tired from the night before; Saturday and Sunday are where the habit shows up. Fill the evenings: a movie, a long walk, a meal out where the drink is not the point. Expect to notice how often you reach for a drink at a set time, such as after work or while cooking.

Week 2: January 8-14

Routine is back and so is work stress. This is when "just one" starts to sound reasonable. Notice the time and place of each urge; a week of notes will show you your two or three main triggers. Our guide to handling alcohol cravings covers what to do in the moment.

Week 3: January 15-21

The middle of the month can feel flat: the novelty is gone and February looks far away. In the US, Monday, January 18 is Martin Luther King Jr. Day, so many people have a three-day weekend. Plan one thing for it you would not do hungover, like an early hike or a morning swim.

Week 4 and the final weekend: January 22-31

Payday falls at the end of the month for many people, and the last weekend is January 30-31. Decide in advance how you'll mark finishing on Sunday night. Many people use this week to decide how they want to drink in February, if at all.

What happens if you slip?

If you do drink, notice what led up to it, and carry on with the next day. A slip on day 12 does not cancel the 11 days before it, and the month is still worth finishing. Write down the situation, the time and who you were with, so next time you see it coming.

What does the research say about Dry January?

Researchers at the University of Sussex, led by Dr. Richard de Visser, surveyed more than 6,000 people through 2019, comparing Dry January participants with adults who didn't take part. Of the participants who replied:

  • 87% had a sense of achievement
  • 84% saved money
  • 80% felt more in control of their drinking
  • 72% slept better

These are self-reported answers from people who chose to take part, so they show what participants noticed, not what a month off will do for any one person. The same study found participants were still drinking less in August than at the start of the year. Alcohol Change UK also says people who use its free tools and emails are twice as likely to complete the 31 days.

Dry January tips that help most

  • Count the days you have done, not the days left. Day 20 of 31 is a better thought than 11 to go.
  • Use a ritual for the time you used to drink. Same glass, different drink.
  • Have a line ready. "I'm doing Dry January" ends most conversations, and in January nobody questions it.
  • Eat before evening events. Being hungry makes it harder to say no.
  • Leave early. Decide your leaving time before you arrive, and go when it comes.

Questions

When does Dry January 2027 start and end?

It starts on Friday, January 1, 2027 and ends on Sunday, January 31, 2027, which makes 31 days. If you finish, your first day back would be Monday, February 1.

Is Dry January 31 days?

Yes. January has 31 days and the challenge covers all of them. If you count from January 1, you reach 30 full days on January 31 and complete 31 days at midnight that night.

Is it safe to stop drinking suddenly for Dry January?

It depends on how much you drink. If you drink heavily every day or are dependent on alcohol, stopping suddenly can cause serious withdrawal, including seizures. In that case, or if you are not sure, talk to a doctor or a local alcohol service first and ask how to stop safely.

What should I do if I drink during Dry January?

Treat it as a slip, not the end. Note what triggered it and continue the next day. Many people restart their count, and some simply mark the day and keep going to January 31.
