How many days sober am I? Counting from your sobriety date

How many days sober am I? Count from your sobriety date, with worked examples, a 30/60/90-day to 1-year milestone table and money-saved math.

Updated 26 Nov 20265 min read

To work out how many days sober you are, subtract your sobriety date (the first day you didn't drink) from today's date. If your sobriety date was January 1, 2027, then on January 31, 2027 you are 30 days sober, and on January 1, 2028 you are one year sober.

The sobriety date: day zero or day one?

Your sobriety date is usually the day after your last drink. If your last drink was on the evening of December 31, your sobriety date is January 1.

On the sobriety date itself you have zero complete days. When that day ends, you have one. Ages and anniversaries are counted the same way, and it is the usual rule for sobriety counters: 30 days sober means 30 whole days have passed since the start.

Some people prefer to call their first sober day "day 1". That is fine for a journal, but it puts you one day ahead of a counter that uses the usual rule. Pick one rule and keep it.

Full 24-hour days or calendar days

There are two common ways a counter turns time into days:

  • Full 24-hour days. One day per 24 hours since your exact start time. If you started at 10 pm, each new day ticks over at 10 pm. This is the stricter count.
  • Calendar days. One day each time the clock passes midnight. If your last drink was at 10 pm, you reach day one two hours later.

The two can differ by one day during part of each day. Neither is wrong. If you share your date with a group or a sponsor, use the rule they use.

Worked example

Say your last drink was at 10 pm on Monday, August 31, 2026 and you start your counter at that moment.

At 9 am on Thursday, November 26, 2026:

  1. Calendar days: August 31 to November 26 is 87 calendar days, so the calendar count reads 87.
  2. Full 24-hour days: from 10 pm on August 31 to 9 am on November 26 is 86 days and 11 hours, so the strict count reads 86.
  3. At 10 pm that evening, the strict count catches up to 87.

The gap only ever covers the hours between midnight and your start time.

Sobriety milestones table: 30, 60 and 90 days to 1 year

Day milestones are counted in days. Month and year milestones follow the calendar: 6 months after the 1st is the 1st, six months later. When the month is shorter, the milestone falls on its last day, so a start on August 31 reaches 6 months on February 28.

Two things people often miss:

  • 30 days and 1 month are not the same day. From January 1, 30 days lands on January 31 but 1 month lands on February 1. February is short, so 60 days from January 1 lands on March 2, not March 1.
  • Leap years move day counts, not anniversaries. 2028 has a February 29. Your one-year date stays on the same calendar day, but a 365-day count and a one-year count drift apart by a day when a February 29 falls inside the year.

How much money have you saved by not drinking?

Multiply what you used to spend on an average day by the number of days sober:

money saved = daily spend × days sober

If you spent about $105 a week on drinks, that is $15 a day:

Days soberMoney kept at $15 a day
30$450
90$1,350
180$2,700
365$5,475

Be honest about the daily figure. Include rounds bought for others, rides home and takeout after a night out, and leave out anything you would have bought anyway. A weekly figure divided by seven is usually more accurate than guessing a daily one.

Common counting mistakes

  • Counting the sobriety date as a full day before it has ended. This puts every milestone one day early.
  • Adding 30 days for each month. Six months is not 180 days; from January 1 it is 181 days.
  • Changing the rule mid-count. If you switch between calendar days and 24-hour days, your count jumps or stalls. Choose once.
  • Forgetting the time zone after travel. A strict 24-hour count does not change when you fly; a midnight count can gain or lose a day.

Questions

How do I calculate my sobriety date?

Your sobriety date is the first full day without a drink, normally the day after your last one. If you aren't sure of the exact day, pick the latest date you are sure of and count from there.

Is my first sober day day 0 or day 1?

Counters usually treat the sobriety date as day 0 and show 1 day once it has ended. Calling it day 1 is also common in journals; the difference is one day, as long as you stay consistent.

If you relapse, do you start over?

Most people reset the count to a new sobriety date after drinking again. The days before still happened: you learned what helped, and many people keep a record of total sober days alongside the current run. The NIAAA's Rethinking Drinking site has a short worksheet on recovering after a slip.

When is my 90 days sober date?

Add 90 days to your sobriety date. From January 1, 2027 that is April 1, 2027; from August 31, 2026 it is November 29, 2026. The milestone table above shows 30, 60 and 90 days, 6 months and 1 year for both.
